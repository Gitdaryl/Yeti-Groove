A storm day at a roofing company

Hail at 3:47. Paid by Friday.

Follow one storm through a roofer's week. A plumber's emergency call, a fence job or a repair order runs the same way, with your steps, your paperwork and your words.

3:47 pm

  1. 3:47 pm

    Hail crosses three of your towns.

    Before the storm has passed, a post for each town it hit is written and waiting for your yes.

  2. 4:20 pm

    You are on a roof when the phone rings.

    The caller gets a text back right away. The voicemail is typed out. The lead is waiting when you climb down.

  3. 6:05 pm

    By dinner, every call, text and form is one list.

    Nobody copies a phone number off a sticky note, and nobody calls back the wrong guy.

  4. Next morning

    Measure, price, and the options on the iPad.

    Good, better, best, plus the add-ons. They pick between your prices instead of calling around.

  5. 10:40 am

    Signed at the kitchen table.

    The contract is filed before you leave the driveway, and the app will not book the crew inside the 3-day cancel window.

  6. Build day, 6:50 am

    The crew talks back, in their own language.

    Miguel says it in Spanish into his phone. The office reads it in English, answers in English, and he reads the answer in Spanish.

  7. 9:15 am

    Rotten decking under the shingles.

    The crew's photo starts the change order, and the homeowner signs it before the extra work.

  8. Friday

    Final walk, invoice, paid.

    Record the check and the job reads paid in full. The receipt goes to the homeowner on your letterhead.

  9. Next week

    The thank-you and the review ask.

    Every customer is asked the same way. A review of 3 stars or less opens a job for someone to call and make it right.

  10. 2:14 am

    And while you sleep, it watches itself.

    The app is checked every 5 minutes. If an update breaks something, it rolls itself back and the studio hears about it first.

Questions

The short answers.

My crew is not good with phones. Will they use it?

If they can send a text, they can use it. They sign in with a phone number and a PIN, in their own language, and see today's job first. We train them on their own phones, and every screen has a ? that plays a short video.

What happens on a roof with no signal?

Keep working. Photos, check-ins and plow visits save on the phone and send themselves when the signal comes back.

We are not roofers. Will it fit us?

Yes. The first company running it does roofing and snow, so that is what you see here. We set it up around your work: a plumber's service call, a mechanic's repair order, a fence or deck job, with your own steps, documents and prices.

We do something our own way. Can it do that?

Probably. Most of what is on this page started as one company asking for it. Tell us how you work and we will show you where it fits.

Can we bring our old jobs over?

Yes. We move your open jobs in from paper, so the crew and the office start with what is already on the books.

Will it text my customers on its own?

No. Every text and email is a draft a person reads and approves before it goes, and it goes out signed by the salesperson on the job.

Who owns our customer list and photos?

Your company does. Export it whenever you want, and it leaves with you if you ever stop.
