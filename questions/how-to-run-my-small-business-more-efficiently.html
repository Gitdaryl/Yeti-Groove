Follow one job

Software shows you how the business is supposed to run. Following one real job shows you how it actually runs. Pick a normal job, not a disaster, and trace it: the call, the quote, the schedule, the materials, the crew, the photos, the invoice, the payment. Every hand-off is a place where something gets retyped, waits, or gets forgotten.

Ask three questions

These are the same three we ask on the form for The Lost Customer Check, because they find most of it:

  • What gets done twice? The same name, address and scope typed into a quote, then the accounting, then the calendar.
  • What sits waiting on one person? The quote only the owner can write. The answer only the office manager knows.
  • What only works because somebody remembers it? The step that isn't written down anywhere, until that person is on vacation.

Keep records in one place

Scattered records cost time every day and real money at tax time. The IRS puts it plainly:

“Good records will help you monitor the progress of your business, prepare your financial statements, identify sources of income, keep track of deductible expenses…”IRS

The SBA says the same about bookkeeping: maintaining it properly can help keep your business running smoothly. One place where each job lives, with its photos, paperwork and money, does more than any new app.

Don't rip out what works

Replacing a system your people already know is expensive and usually the wrong answer. Most of the lost time lives in the gaps between tools: the estimate that doesn't become the job, the photos that don't leave the phone. Connect those first.

Then sort what's left. Some work can run on its own. Some should go straight to the right person. The judgement calls, like pricing a tricky job, stay with you, with the numbers lined up so it takes ten minutes instead of an evening.
