What happens when a customer calls my business after hours?

Short answerCall your own number at seven tonight and find out. If it rings out or the voicemail is full, that customer is waiting on you while other businesses are a tap away. Make sure every after-hours call gets a quick reply: a text back within seconds, or an answering service or voice assistant that takes their details for the morning.

YetiGroove Studio · Updated November 7, 2026

Test it yourself

Most owners have never heard their own business from the outside after hours. It takes two minutes:

  • Call your main number after closing. Does it ring, go to voicemail, or say the box is full?
  • Listen to the greeting. Does it say when you will call back?
  • Fill in your own website form. How long until anyone sees it?
  • Message your Facebook page. Who gets the notification?

Why speed matters

A well-known 2011 Harvard Business Review study timed how 2,241 U.S. companies answered a web inquiry. 23% never answered at all, and the average reply took 42 hours. A second study in the same article found companies that tried to make contact within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to reach a decision maker as those that tried an hour later.

A call at 7 pm that gets answered at 9 the next morning is already 14 hours behind.

Keep your hours honest on Google

People check your hours on Google before they call. Google suggests confirming your hours for holidays even if they don't change.

“You can set special hours if your business temporarily adjusts its hours of operation or remains closed for up to 6 days in a row.”Google Business Profile Help

If you'll be closed seven days or more in a row, Google says to mark the business as temporarily closed instead.

Before you set up automatic texts

A missed-call text back is one of the simplest fixes there is, but texting from a business system has rules.

“Anyone sending SMS/MMS messages over a 10DLC number from an application to the US must register for A2P 10DLC.”Twilio Docs

Twilio says registered numbers get less filtering, and unregistered ones pay extra carrier fees. The phone industry's own guidelines ask for standard STOP wording so people can opt out, and one final confirmation once they do.

When we set this up, we register the number, keep the reply to answering the customer's own call, and include opt-out wording. If texts are part of your marketing too, have the consent wording on your forms checked.
