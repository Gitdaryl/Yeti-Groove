The part most people skip

Anything we leave running, we watch. Automations break quietly. A password expires, a service changes a rule, and something that worked for eight months stops without telling anybody. So every system we install comes with a monthly care plan and a nightly health check that tells us first.

We are not a tech company. We make things.

Good. The businesses that gain the most are the least technical ones, because nobody has ever looked at their back office with fresh eyes. Contractors, fabricators, marinas, restaurants. If you can walk us through a job, we can find the leaks.

Are you going to tell us to replace our software?

Almost certainly not. Ripping out a system your people already know is expensive and usually the wrong answer. Most of what we find lives in the gaps between the tools you already pay for.

Is this an AI thing?

Some of it, where it earns its place. Most of it is more boring than that: connecting two things that should already have been connected. The goal is fewer hours, not more technology.

Who owns what you build?

Your information is yours, always. The automation we build on audit day is yours to keep whether or not we work together again.

What if you find nothing?

Then we say so, you keep the automation we built on the day, and we do not send you a quote for work that would not pay for itself.
