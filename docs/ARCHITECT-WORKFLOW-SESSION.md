## Why charge for it

The fee is the filter. It separates a man who will change his process from a man
collecting app names. It also sets the frame before minute one: this is craft, not a
tool list. Crediting it back makes yes free if he ever hires the studio.

Never run this free. A free version becomes a 40 minute app list, he tries it alone,
it looks like garbage, and he quietly concludes the studio's work was luck.

## The belief you are up against

Almost everyone arrives believing **app plus prompt equals magic delivered**. He is
paying, in part, to have that corrected by someone who is not selling him software.

Correct it honestly and early. Not to protect pricing, but because it is true, and
because a man who believes it is easy will burn a month proving it is not.

| What he probably believes | What is actually true |
|---|---|
| One app does it | Six to eight tools in a fixed sequence. No single one gets there |
| The prompt is the skill | The prompt is maybe 20%. Sequence, selection, and grading are the rest |
| First output is the output | Real ratio is roughly 1 keeper in 6 to 10 generations |
| It is free or near free | Per finished minute there is real render spend, and it compounds with every retry |
| AI replaced the labor | The labor moved. Less rendering time, far more judgment and selection time |
| It will match my plans | Models do not read plans. Holding true to his elevations is the hardest part of the job |

The last row is the one that matters most to an architect, and it is the row that
sells the Previz Pack. His whole professional value is that the thing gets built the
way he drew it. Generic AI will happily give him a beautiful house that is not his.

**The line to land it:** "The tools got cheap. The judgment did not. What you are
looking at in that Devils Lake film is not a prompt, it is a hundred rejected takes."

## Prep before the session (studio side)

1. Get 1 real project from him in advance: plans, elevations, site plan, and any
   existing renders. Ask on the discovery call so it is in hand.
2. Pre-generate the good version of one of his scenes. Do not show it yet.
3. Pull his current-method artifacts so the comparison is his work, not a stranger's.
4. Have the naive prompt ready to run live, unrehearsed, on camera.
5. Recording on, local. Confirm consent at the top.

## Run of show

### 0 to 10 - The honest frame
Set expectations before any screen. Walk the table above. Say plainly what did not
get easier. Ending on "the labor moved, it did not vanish" buys credibility for
everything after it.

### 10 to 25 - The contrast, live
The most important 15 minutes. Do not describe it, run it.

1. Type the naive prompt for one of HIS scenes. The kind of prompt he would write.
2. Generate. Show the result cold, including the bad ones. Do not curate.
3. Name the failure modes out loud as they appear: wrong roof pitch, invented windows,
   melted trim, camera drifting through a wall, the house that is not his house.
4. Then reveal the pre-generated good version of the same scene.
5. Ask him what he thinks is between those two images. Let him answer. Then show him
   it is the next 45 minutes.

Right-versus-wrong side by side, not prose. He is a visual professional. Show him.

### 25 to 50 - The sequence
The actual pipeline, stage by stage, in order, with the reason each stage exists.

- **Plan fidelity first.** Establishing image locked to his elevations before anything
  moves. Reference images beat description every time. This is where an architect wins
  and a hobbyist loses, because he has the drawings
- **Scene, not shot.** Decide the six moments that sell the building before generating
  a single frame. Scene count is the unit of work and the unit of cost
- **Stills before motion.** Never animate an image you are not already happy with.
  Motion multiplies whatever is wrong in the frame
- **Reference locking.** How the same house stays the same house across all six scenes.
  Consistency is the whole game and it is not automatic
- **Motion pass.** Image to video, one move per clip. What holds and what falls apart
- **Selection.** The unglamorous stage nobody talks about. Generate wide, cut hard
- **Grade and finish.** One pass over everything so six clips look like one film.
  Ungraded AI footage reads as AI footage no matter how good the frames are

### 50 to 70 - The rules that cost money to learn
This is the paid part. Real, specific, hard-won.

- **Containment.** A camera move must resolve or the tension dies. State that the move
  holds. Unresolved pull-backs read as broken
- **One move per clip.** Two moves in one generation is how you get slop
- **Draft cheap, finish expensive.** Route volume drafts to the cheap pipe, route
  anything that ships to the pipe that holds the palette. Cheap pipes shift color
- **Render smaller, upscale after.** Roughly a third cheaper than rendering native
  high res, and you land at 4K. One upscale pass only, never stack them
- **Model routing.** Different engines for stills, motion, and mechanical subjects.
  Naming which is which is a footnote, knowing why is the skill
- **Label everything as a visualization of approved plans.** Protects him, protects
  his client, and it is simply correct. Say this out loud, architects care

Caveat to give him honestly: specific tool names churn every few months. The sequence
and the rules survive. Teach him the durable half.

### 70 to 85 - His project, on the table
Take the real project he sent. Together, block out the six scenes that would sell it.
Do this as a working session, not a demo. He should be doing most of the talking.

He now has something he did not have this morning: a scene plan for a live job.

### 85 to 90 - The fork, stated plainly
Two honest doors, no pressure, no closing energy:

- **Do it in house.** Here is what it costs him. Realistic hours to first usable pack,
  the subscription spend, and the learning curve before output is client-ready. Be
  accurate even where accurate is discouraging
- **Hand it over.** Architect Previz Pack, $2,900, 6 scenes plus a 30 second flythrough,
  8 business days, billed through to his client as a line item like any rendering.
  The $750 today comes straight off it

Then stop talking. Most principals who sit through the middle 45 minutes do the math
themselves and pick Door 2. Let them.
