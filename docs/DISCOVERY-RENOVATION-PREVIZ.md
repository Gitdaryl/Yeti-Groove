# Discovery Sheet - Renovation Previz (existing building, member/board client)

Companion to DISCOVERY-CALL-SHEET.md, which covers raw-land developer projects.
Use this one when the client is an existing institution renovating a building they
already own: clubs, churches, lodges, restaurants, municipal buildings.

Report answers to Claude after the meeting. Claude scopes from docs/SIGNATURE-RATE-CARD.md.

## Before you walk in: the $2,000 anchor

Devils Lake Cove paid $2,000 and told everyone, DLYC included. The number is in the
room before you are. Raise it yourself in the first five minutes - whoever says it
first decides what it means.

**The line:** "You've probably heard what the Cove paid. I'd rather you hear it from
me. That was the first film of its kind I built, and I priced it to find out what it
actually took. Now I know. Dave has told me he'd have paid thirty thousand for it in
California. Today the number is seventy-five hundred, and here's exactly what that buys."

Why it works: it is true, it makes the $2,000 a fact about the learning curve rather
than leverage, and it puts a peer's $30k valuation in the room instead of your own claim.

Do not apologize. Do not say "special deal." Do not open below $7,500.

**Pre-meeting action:** ask Dave whether he'll say the $30k line to them directly, or
give you a one-sentence testimonial you can show. A peer beats any claim you make.

**The standing lesson:** never again price quietly with "don't tell anyone." In a lake
town everybody talks. If a job needs to be cheap, make it a smaller SKU with a public
price, not a secret discount.

## Before you walk in: the tier trap

A renovation story wants before/after. Before/after means a camera on the existing
clubhouse. Per the rate card, a camera on site makes this a **Signature Film,
from $7,500** - not the $2,900 Architect Previz Pack.

The Previz Pack is not a cheaper version of the film. It is a channel SKU: plans in,
scenes out, no shoot, no score. **Never offer it to the club directly.**

The escape hatch if budget is genuinely the wall: **the architect buys the Previz Pack
and bills it through to the club**, exactly as that SKU is designed. The club sees a
smaller number, the rate card stays intact, and you open a repeatable channel. It runs
through the architect, never through the room.

## 1. What is the previz actually for?

Not "what do you want" - what decision does it have to move?

- [ ] Member vote on an assessment
- [ ] Capital campaign / donor asks
- [ ] Bank or lender package
- [ ] Township / permit / zoning approval
- [ ] Excitement only, decision already made

Each one changes the film. A vote film answers "what do I get for my money."
A lender film is sober and dimensioned. A permit film is accurate and unglamorous.
A campaign film shows naming opportunities. Ask which, then build to it.

## 2. Who decides, and when?

- Board vote or full membership vote?
- **Date of that meeting.** That is the real deadline, not "sometime this fall."
- Who signs the contract, and does signing require its own vote?
- One named approver for creative, or you get a committee revision spiral.

## 3. What exists on paper?

- Architect or design-build firm engaged? **Get the name.** An architect on this job
  is a channel relationship worth more than the single project - Previz Pack, repeatable.
- Approved plans, elevations, site plan? What format (PDF / CAD / Revit / SketchUp)?
- Who supplies them, and by when? Delivery clock starts at plan handoff, not signature.
- **If no plans exist yet, previz is premature.** Say so. Concept work without approved
  plans is a different, riskier job and gets priced accordingly.

## 4. Scope of the renovation

- What is physically changing? (deck, dining room, bar, kitchen, docks, exterior, parking)
- Phases? Which phase is being funded right now?
- Which spaces MUST appear on screen? **This is your scene count.** Signature Film
  includes up to 8. Scene 9 is $450 and always quoted.

## 5. Before / after

- Do they want the building as it stands today on camera? (Almost always yes.)
  Confirms Film tier, see the trap above.
- **Season pressure:** the lake and the grounds show best right now, late August.
  If the vote is in spring, you shoot NOW or you shoot bare trees and grey water.
  This is your honest urgency lever - use it, it is real.

## 6. Where does it show?

Member meeting projector, website, email blast, fundraising dinner, local press,
social. Drives the cut list and usage terms. Vertical cut needed?

## 7. Timeline

- Hard date is the vote or campaign launch
- Signature Film delivers 2-3 weeks from plan handoff
- Rush is +30%, quote it rather than compressing quietly

## 8. Money

- Where does it come from: operating funds, capital reserve, campaign pledges?
- Present Signature Film from $7,500, then Development Package from $15,000
- Never quote below $7,500

## Club-specific watch-outs

- **Committee creep.** A dozen board members with opinions will eat your margin.
  One named approver in the contract. Two revision rounds, then $300/round.
- **Do not get treated like a rendering vendor.** They may have priced renderings
  from the architect. This is a film that moves a vote. Different product.
- **Off-plan protection.** Every render presented as a visualization of approved plans.
- **Membership sensitivity.** If dues or an assessment are involved, avoid anything
  that reads as luxury spending. Frame around preservation and use, not opulence.
